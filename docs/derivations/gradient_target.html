The gradient target for DT-FBSDE

Differentiating the noiseless Taylor target to get a value-function slope target for every sample, and why the Taylor Q-function policy needs it.

Result

For a sample \(x\) at stage \(i\), with the minimizing control \(u^\star\) and landing point \(\varphi = x + f_i(x) + B_i(x)\,u^\star\):

$$\hat G_i(x) \;=\; \nabla_x \hat Y_i(x) \;\approx\; J_\varphi(x, u^\star)^{\top}\, \nabla V_{i+1}(\varphi), \qquad J_\varphi = I + \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial \big(B_i(x)\,u^\star\big)}{\partial x}.$$

It is the discrete costate (adjoint) recursion, it is exact when \(V_{i+1}\) is quadratic and the dynamics are linear, and \(Z_i = \sigma_i^{\top} \hat G_i\) in FBSDE terms.

0Setup: the pieces as the solver has them

The forward model is a control-affine step with standard normal noise \(w\):

$$x_{i+1} = x + f_i(x) + B_i(x)\,u + \Sigma_i(x)\,w, \qquad w \sim \mathcal N(0, I), \qquad \Sigma_i = \sigma_i \sqrt{\Delta t}.$$

Write the noise-free landing point under control \(u\) as

$$\varphi_i(x, u) = x + f_i(x) + B_i(x)\,u .$$

The running cost is L1 in the control and does not depend on the state, \(\ell_i(x, u) = c\,\lVert u\rVert_1\) with \(c = c_0\,\Delta t\) (here \(c = 0.0625\)), over the box \(u \in [-1, 1]^M\).

The expected next-stage value is replaced by its second-order Taylor expansion about the landing point. With \(\mathbb E[w] = 0\) and \(\mathbb E[w w^{\top}] = I\),

$$\mathbb E\big[V_{i+1}(\varphi + \Sigma w)\big] \approx V_{i+1}(\varphi) + \nabla V_{i+1}(\varphi)^{\top}\Sigma\,\mathbb E[w] + \tfrac12\,\mathbb E\big[w^{\top}\Sigma^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}(\varphi)\,\Sigma w\big] = V_{i+1}(\varphi) + \tfrac12 \operatorname{tr}\!\big(\Sigma^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}(\varphi)\,\Sigma\big).$$

That gives the Taylor Q-function (thesis eq. 4.65) the policy minimizes, and the value target the backward pass fits:

$$\tilde Q_i(x, u) = c\,\lVert u\rVert_1 + V_{i+1}\big(\varphi_i(x,u)\big) + \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\!\Big(\Sigma_i^{\top}\,\nabla^2 V_{i+1}\big(\varphi_i(x,u)\big)\,\Sigma_i\Big),$$ $$u^\star(x) = \arg\min_{u \in [-1,1]^M} \tilde Q_i(x, u), \qquad \hat Y_i(x) = \tilde Q_i\big(x, u^\star(x)\big).$$

Today the regression fits \(V_i(x_j) \approx \hat Y_i(x_j)\) and nothing else. The goal here is a second target, \(\hat G_i(x) \approx \nabla V_i(x)\), computed as cheaply and as noise-free as \(\hat Y_i\).

1Why the policy runs on slopes, not values

The policy compares \(\tilde Q_i(x,u)\) across candidate controls at one fixed state. Every candidate lands on the same line through \(x + f_i(x)\), spaced along the control column \(b = B_i(x)\):

Comparing two candidates at the same state, to first order,

$$\tilde Q_i(x, u + \Delta u) - \tilde Q_i(x, u) \approx c\big(\lVert u+\Delta u\rVert_1 - \lVert u\rVert_1\big) + \Delta u\; b^{\top}\nabla V_{i+1}(\varphi) .$$

So the decision is a contest between the fuel price \(c\) and the slope \(b^{\top}\nabla V_{i+1}\). In continuous time it is exactly the bang-off-bang rule \(u^\star = -\operatorname{sign}(b^{\top}\nabla V)\) when \(|b^{\top}\nabla V| > c\), else \(0\). A fit with small value error but the wrong slope along \(b\) flips these decisions, and nothing in a value-only regression constrains that slope.

2Differentiate the target: the chain rule

\(\hat Y_i\) depends on \(x\) directly and through the control it chose:

$$\nabla_x \hat Y_i(x) = \underbrace{\nabla_x \tilde Q_i(x, u)\Big|_{u = u^\star(x)}}_{\text{at fixed } u} \;+\; \Big(\frac{\partial u^\star}{\partial x}\Big)^{\!\top} \nabla_u \tilde Q_i\big(x, u^\star(x)\big).$$

3The control's own derivative drops out (envelope argument)

Split \(\tilde Q_i = c\lVert u\rVert_1 + S_i(x, u)\) with \(S_i\) smooth. Take any open set of states on which the minimizer keeps the same pattern, and look at one control coordinate \(j\):

where \(u^\star_j\) sitswhat vanishes
at a bound, \(u^\star_j = \pm 1\)\(\partial u^\star_j/\partial x = 0\): it stays at the bound nearby
at the kink, \(u^\star_j = 0\)\(\partial u^\star_j/\partial x = 0\): it coasts nearby
interior, \(0 < |u^\star_j| < 1\)\(\partial \tilde Q_i/\partial u_j = c\,\operatorname{sign}(u^\star_j) + \partial S_i/\partial u_j = 0\): the first-order condition

Every term of \(\sum_j (\partial u^\star_j/\partial x)\,\partial \tilde Q_i/\partial u_j\) has one zero factor, so the second term of step 2 is zero. (In the interior case \(u^\star_j\) is differentiable by the implicit function theorem when \(\partial^2 S_i/\partial u_j^2 > 0\), which the policy requires before it uses a stationary point.) The states where the pattern switches form surfaces of measure zero; \(\hat Y_i\) is continuous across them but has a kink, so the gradient exists almost everywhere. Hence

$$\nabla_x \hat Y_i(x) = \nabla_x \tilde Q_i(x, u)\Big|_{u = u^\star(x)} \quad \text{almost everywhere.}$$

4Differentiate at fixed control, term by term

The fuel term has no \(x\) in it: \(\nabla_x\, c\lVert u\rVert_1 = 0\).

The value term, by the chain rule through the landing point:

$$\nabla_x\, V_{i+1}\big(\varphi_i(x,u)\big) = J_\varphi^{\top}\,\nabla V_{i+1}(\varphi), \qquad J_\varphi = \frac{\partial \varphi_i}{\partial x} = I + \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x} + \sum_{m=1}^{M} u_m \frac{\partial b_m}{\partial x},$$

where \(b_m\) is the \(m\)-th column of \(B_i\).

The noise-curvature term \(T(x) = \tfrac12 \operatorname{tr}\big(\Sigma_i^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}(\varphi)\Sigma_i\big)\), for a constant diffusion (both benchmarks), changes with \(x\) only through \(\varphi\):

$$\nabla_x T = \tfrac12\, J_\varphi^{\top}\, \nabla_\varphi \operatorname{tr}\!\big(\Sigma_i^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}(\varphi)\,\Sigma_i\big), \qquad \Big[\nabla_\varphi \operatorname{tr}(\cdot)\Big]_k = \sum_{a,b} \big(\Sigma_i\Sigma_i^{\top}\big)_{ab}\, \frac{\partial^3 V_{i+1}}{\partial \varphi_a\,\partial \varphi_b\,\partial \varphi_k}.$$

(A state-dependent diffusion would add \(\operatorname{tr}\big(\Sigma_i^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}\,\partial\Sigma_i/\partial x_k\big)\) to each component.) Altogether, exactly:

$$\hat G_i(x) = J_\varphi^{\top}\Big[\nabla V_{i+1}(\varphi) + \tfrac12\,\nabla_\varphi \operatorname{tr}\!\big(\Sigma_i^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}(\varphi)\,\Sigma_i\big)\Big]_{u = u^\star(x)} .$$

5Drop the third-derivative term

The bracket's second part is of size \(\lVert\Sigma_i\rVert^2\,\lVert\nabla^3 V_{i+1}\rVert\). It vanishes when \(V_{i+1}\) is quadratic, which is exactly the case where the Taylor estimator itself is exact, and keeping it would carry the expansion past the second order that \(\tilde Q_i\) was built at. With \(\Sigma_i \sim 0.1\sqrt{\Delta t}\) here, it is small. Dropping it:

$$\hat G_i(x) \approx J_\varphi\big(x, u^\star(x)\big)^{\top}\;\nabla V_{i+1}\big(\varphi_i(x, u^\star(x))\big).$$

6What it is, and a check it must pass

The costate recursion. Writing \(\lambda_i = \nabla V_i\) along a trajectory, this is \(\lambda_i = A_i^{\top}\lambda_{i+1}\) with \(A_i = J_\varphi\): the discrete Pontryagin adjoint, and the gradient half of a DDP backward pass. It is anchored at the terminal stage, where \(\lambda_K = \nabla\) of the terminal cost exactly, just as \(\hat Y\) is anchored by the terminal cost.

The Z process. In continuous time \(Z_t = \sigma^{\top}\nabla V\), so with \(\Sigma_i = \sigma_i\sqrt{\Delta t}\), \(Z_i = \sigma_i^{\top}\hat G_i\). Like \(\hat Y_i\), this estimate reads no noise realization; the usual least-squares Monte Carlo alternative, \(Z_i \approx \mathbb E[\hat Y_{i+1}\,\Delta W_i]/\Delta t\), has variance growing like \(1/\Delta t\) and does not suit small batches.

The exactness check. Take linear dynamics \(\varphi = A x + B u\) and a quadratic \(V_{i+1}(y) = \tfrac12 y^{\top}P y + p^{\top}y + r\). Then \(\nabla^2 V_{i+1} = P\) is constant, the trace term is a constant, \(J_\varphi = A\), and

$$\hat G_i(x) = A^{\top}\big(P\,\varphi + p\big) = \nabla_x \hat Y_i(x) \quad \text{a.e., with no approximation.}$$

That makes a unit test: on the double integrator with a quadratic table, \(\hat G_i\) must match a central finite difference of \(\hat Y_i\) away from switching surfaces.

7Computing it on the two benchmarks

Double integrator. Zero-order hold of a unit mass with \(\Delta t = 1/16\):

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \Delta t \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix} \Delta t^2/2 \\ \Delta t \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat G_i(x) = A^{\top}\,\nabla V_{i+1}(A x + b\,u^\star).$$

Pendulum. \(\ddot\theta = a_1\dot\theta + a_2\sin\theta + a_3 u\), stepped by RK4. To first order in \(\Delta t\), for intuition only (the code should use the RK4 step itself):

$$J_\varphi \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \Delta t \\ a_2\Delta t\cos\theta & 1 + a_1\Delta t \end{pmatrix}, \qquad b \approx \begin{pmatrix} a_3\Delta t^2/2 \\ a_3\Delta t \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.008 \\ 0.25 \end{pmatrix}.$$

In the solver. No model exposes a Jacobian today, so take it by central differences of the noise-free step at the chosen control, \(\varphi_i(x,u) = \) forward_model(i, x, u, 0):

$$J_\varphi\,e_k \approx \frac{\varphi_i(x + h e_k,\, u^\star) - \varphi_i(x - h e_k,\, u^\star)}{2h},$$

\(2N\) model calls per sample, then \(\hat G_i = J_\varphi^{\top}\) times the next stage's Gradient() at \(\varphi\). With double estimation, use the scoring half's gradient at the choosing half's \(u^\star\), the same pairing the value target uses.

8Using it: a fit that constrains the slope

Add a gradient residual to the regression, projected on the direction the previous stage's policy will probe \(V_i\) along, \(b = B_{i-1}\) (nearly constant on both benchmarks):

$$\min_\theta \sum_j w_j\Big[\big(V_\theta(x_j) - \hat Y_i(x_j)\big)^2 + \gamma\,\big\lVert b^{\top}\big(\nabla V_\theta(x_j) - \hat G_i(x_j)\big)\big\rVert^2\Big].$$

\(b\) is the change in state per unit of control, and the control is dimensionless, so \(b^{\top}\nabla V\) is in cost units: it is the value difference across one full control step. The projected residual is therefore directly comparable with the decision threshold \(c = 0.0625\), and \(\gamma \approx 1\) is the natural starting weight; the check in step 9 found \(\gamma \approx 10\) works best. (This corrects my earlier remark that \(\gamma\) should be about \(1/\lVert B\rVert^2\): that holds for an unprojected gradient residual, not this one.)

For the polynomial model \(\nabla V_\theta\) is linear in \(\theta\), so this stays a closed-form least squares with extra rows. For the soft-min model, SGD needs \(\partial(\nabla V_\theta)/\partial\theta\), which SoftMinQuadraticValueFunctionApprox does not provide yet.

9Does a slope target help? A ceiling check

Before building \(\hat G_i\) into the solver, the pendulum diagnostic (l1_pendulum_gap_diagnosis --sobolev-only) fits the polynomial model in closed form to the optimal value function's values and to its slope along \(b\) (central differences of the grid optimum), weighting the samples off the optimal paths by 0.01, and scores the Taylor Q-function policy over the fit on 1024 held-out rollouts. The grid optimum costs 2.272, and the same policy over the exact optimal value function costs 2.286 (0.6% above).

The best weight here is \(\gamma \approx 10\), not the \(\gamma \approx 1\) suggested in step 8, and at \(\gamma = 100\) the policy breaks: its terminal cost jumps from 0.05 to 0.9 and 3.3 while the fit error on the paths stays at 0.05, so the slope term is then overriding the values somewhere the paths do not cover. The weight needs tuning, not a units argument.

These slope targets come from the exact optimal value function: this is the ceiling. In the solver, \(\hat G_i\) is built from the fitted \(\nabla V_{i+1}\), so its errors propagate backward, and the solver check has still to be run.

Caveats

  • Double estimation. The control is chosen by one half's table and scored by the other's, so \(\nabla_u \tilde Q\) of the scorer is not exactly zero at an interior control. The leftover term is the product of two small errors, but it is not zero.
  • Policy-evaluation targets. With kCurrentPolicy, \(u = \mu(x)\) comes from a different table, and the term \((\partial\mu/\partial x)^{\top}\nabla_u\tilde Q\) survives wherever \(\mu\) is interior. The runs so far used kDoubleGreedy.
  • The target floor. Where \(\max(\hat Y_i, 0)\) clamps, the gradient target is zero.
  • Error propagation. \(\hat G_i\) uses the fitted \(\nabla V_{i+1}\), so slope errors propagate backward as value errors do; the difference is that slopes are now supervised at all.
  • Curvature. Interior controls also depend on \(b^{\top}\nabla^2 V_{i+1}\,b\), which a gradient target does not constrain directly.
