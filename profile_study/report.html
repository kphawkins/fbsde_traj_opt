Where DT-FBSDE spends its time

The default DT-FBSDE configuration (the batch sweep's best 2-D recipe, as run by method_comparison and value_model_study) profiled on the double integrator and the pendulum swing-up, then on the double inverted pendulum, where the picture changes: there the single-threaded SGD fit in the backward pass takes two thirds of the time.

Profiled 2026-10-24 · 2-D: 4 seeds × 60 s each · DIP: 3 + 2 seeds × 120 s · phase timers + perf sampling

Summary

  1. The line search is 70–80% of every 2-D iteration, and most of that is the 32-rollout screening pass. The forward pass is under 11% and the backward pass under 20%.
  2. Screening runs on a single core. ParallelFor gives each thread at least 32 items, so a 32-rollout screen never splits. Screening alone is about two thirds of wall time on both 2-D problems.
  3. Almost all CPU goes to choosing controls. One TaylorQFunctionL1Policy::Decide costs about 13 µs and calls the polynomial's value-gradient-Hessian 19 times. Derivatives<true> alone is 76–88% of CPU samples. The dynamics, the samplers and the 28×28 least-squares fit are each under 5%.
  4. Letting the screening rollouts use every core doubles throughput. A one-line grain change in EvaluatePolicyByRollouts took the 2-D iterations from 112–122 ms to 61–63 ms with identical trajectories.
  5. Most iterations are rejected. Only 7% of iterations accept a step. A typical iteration screens two or three candidate steps, sees the excess rising and stops, so the screening cost is paid every iteration.

Where the time could come back

Run the screening rollouts in parallel measured: 1.8–2.0× faster

Screening runs on one core because ParallelFor keeps at least 32 items per thread (kMinimumPerThread). A 64-stage rollout costs about 0.8 ms, far more than starting a thread, so I tried a grain of 4 rollouts per thread in EvaluatePolicyByRollouts only (a template parameter on ParallelFor, everything else unchanged). On seeds 0 and 1 with the same 60 s budget:

ProblemBeforeAfterIterations in 60 s
Double integrator122 ms/it61 ms/it492 → 983
Pendulum swing-up112 ms/it63 ms/it539 → 955

The trajectories are unchanged: the solver's cost at every iteration both runs reached matched exactly (2,061 iterations compared), since each rollout's noise is addressed by its index. Screening falls from about two thirds of wall time to 34–43%. On a machine with more cores the gain is larger.

Make each control decision cheaper ~76–88% of CPU

Decide runs 3 coordinate sweeps, each with a gradient, a Hessian, and 5 candidate Q-values, and every Q-value calls both operator() and Hessian(), so the polynomial's derivatives are rebuilt 19 times per decision. With one control, the bound and zero candidates land on the same states in every sweep, and the "current" candidate repeats a value already known, so caching those and taking value and Hessian from one ValueGradientAndHessian call would roughly halve the calls. A specialized Derivatives (precomputed exponent and coefficient tables, no per-term array copies) would cut the cost of each call as well. This speeds up the forward pass, the backward targets and every rollout together.

Pay less for rejected iterations algorithmic

93% of iterations accept nothing, and a typical iteration screens two or three candidates to learn that. Cheaper rejection (fewer screening rollouts, or reusing the previous iteration's screened excess) would trade evaluation noise for speed; it changes the algorithm, so it needs a convergence study rather than a profile.

Note the uncharged held-out evaluations study wall time only

The comparison scores 1,024 held-out rollouts at each of about 80 checkpoints in a 60 s run, about 0.22 s each, so roughly 17 s of extra wall time per run. It is not charged to the solver, but it adds about 30% to how long a study takes.

Double inverted pendulum

Two configurations. The standard one is what method_comparison runs by default (--dip-dt-config comparison): 4,096 samples, 101 stages, soft-min value functions with 4 components fitted by 50 epochs of SGD, no screening, no slope matching. The second carries the 2-D recipe over (--dip-dt-config sweep): 256 samples, screening, 6× inflation on half the paths, slope weight 10. Seeds 0–2 (standard) and 0–1 (2-D recipe), 120 s each.

  1. The bottleneck moves from the line search to the backward fit. On the standard configuration the backward pass is 85% of wall time and the SGD fit alone is 66%. The line search is under 4%.
  2. The SGD fit runs on one thread. Each iteration fits 200 tables (100 stages × 2 halves), each about 30 ms of single-threaded SGD over 2,048 samples. The two halves of a stage are independent, so fitting them concurrently would cut the fit's wall time up to 2×; parallelizing the minibatch gradient would go further.
  3. Across all cores, fitting and deciding share the work. In CPU samples the SGD fitter is 43% and control decisions (soft-min value, gradient and Hessian inside Decide) 39%. The pendulum's RK integration (IntegrateReferenceStep, sin, sincos) is about 21%, much of it the drift evaluations inside those decisions.
  4. The 2-D recipe is 7× cheaper per iteration and has the same bottleneck. At 1.3 s per iteration its fit is 61% and its targets 25% of wall time; the slope targets (a finite-difference Jacobian through the RK step) are 62% of target thread time, about 15% of wall time. Screening is under 6% here, because the backward pass dominates.

Method

  • Configuration: SweepDtFbsdeOptions from method_comparison_main.cpp, which is DtFbsdeOptions (double-greedy targets, entropy weights, ε 1 → 0.03) plus the screened stop-uphill line search, 6× noise inflation on half the paths, and slope weight 10 (integrator) or 1 (pendulum). 16 samples per batch, 65 stages, degree-6 polynomial value functions, 256 evaluation rollouts with 32 screening rollouts.
  • Runs: seeds 0–3 of the comparison's seed scheme, 60 s of solver time each, the comparison's 2-D budget. Held-out evaluation was timed separately and not charged, as in the comparison.
  • Timers: the phase profiler in fbsde_traj_opt/utils/phase_profiler.hpp wraps each phase and sub-phase. Each thread keeps its own totals, and they are merged when the thread exits. A profiled and an unprofiled build ran at 0.130 and 0.132 s per iteration on the same seed, so the timers cost less than the run-to-run noise. method_comparison --profile-csv PATH turns them on.
  • Build: g++ 13.3, -O2 -DNDEBUG -g -fno-omit-frame-pointer, Eigen 5.0.0, on a 4-vCPU cloud container. Bazel could not reach its module registry from the container, so this is not the repository's clang toolchain; absolute times will differ on your machine, the shares should not move much. More cores would shrink the parallel full evaluation but not the single-threaded screening.
  • The results, the scripts that build this page, and a README are in profile_study/ on the research branch. The faster screening rollouts (a grain of 4 rollouts per thread) were an experiment and have not been merged.
